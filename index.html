Creator Growth Lab

Helping creators find their next step toward $100

A self-initiated project built around Buy Me a Coffee.

The idea

Creators already have tools to earn through Buy Me a Coffee: support, memberships, Shop and posts. But having more options doesn’t always make growth easier.

The problem isn’t always knowing what is possible. It’s knowing what to do next.

I wanted to explore a simple way to help early-stage creators identify their biggest obstacle, test a solution and learn from the result.

This is an independent hypothesis and prototype, not a representation of Buy Me a Coffee’s internal data or roadmap.

The goal isn’t to give everyone the same advice. It’s to help each creator find their next best action.

From guessing to testing

I designed a simple experiment loop:

Goal→Hypothesis→Test→Measure→Learn

Every experiment ends with one decision:

ContinueIterateStop

I would start with three experiments:

Improve the askTest a clearer support message for creators who have visitors but few supporters.
Test membershipTry one simple membership offer for creators with repeat supporters.
Connect content to actionTrack which posts and calls to action generate support.

I’d start with the first because it’s simple to run and has a clear success metric.

A small example

Imagine Alex gets 500 page visitors a month, but only five become supporters. That’s a 1% conversion rate.

Instead of chasing more traffic, Alex tests a clearer support message.

Before1.0%5 supporters from 500 visitors
If the test works1.6%8 supporters from the same 500

That’s a promising signal to investigate, not proof of causation. If nothing changes, Alex tests another approach.

Alex and all figures are illustrative.

What I’d do next

  1. Validate. Talk to creators to understand where they get stuck.
  2. Experiment. Test the support message and measure conversion.
  3. Learn. Record what worked, what didn’t and why.
  4. Expand. Explore memberships and content experiments if the evidence supports them.

A lightweight tracker would capture each creator’s goal, experiment, baseline, result and next decision.

Why this project

This project brings together audience growth, product thinking, experimentation and operations.

It demonstrates how I’d turn an unclear growth problem into a practical process: understand the obstacle, test a small change, measure the outcome and decide what to do next.

The principle is simple: less guessing, smaller experiments and clearer next steps.

An independent prototype to be refined through creator feedback and real-world evidence.
